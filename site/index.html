Five things AuthScope does, from the toolbar to the dashboard.

How it works

  1. Install it

    Add AuthScope to Chrome or Firefox. It opens its dashboard so you can get started.

  2. Check your accounts

    Click Check all. AuthScope reads the apps linked to each account you're signed in to and adds them to your list, with their websites.

  3. Browse as usual

    The toolbar icon shows which provider you used on each site. New sign-ins and sign-ups are added on their own.

Privacy

AuthScope keeps a list of which sites you used which sign-in provider for. That list never leaves your browser.

Last updated 30 October 2026.

What it stores
Site names and addresses, the provider you used for each (for example Google or GitHub), and when. They're kept in your browser's own extension storage.
What it doesn't store
Passwords, passkeys, email addresses, or your browsing history. It doesn't run any script on the sites you visit.
What it sends
Nothing. There are no servers, accounts, ads or analytics. To show provider icons, it loads each provider's small site icon from that provider's website. When you click Check, it opens your providers' own connected-apps pages, signed in as you, and reads the app names there.
Your control
Remove any site from the dashboard, export your list as a backup or spreadsheet, or delete everything at once. Uninstalling AuthScope deletes its data.
